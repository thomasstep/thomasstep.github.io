I haven’t written anything here in a long time. Over a year. Even before my last post here, my writing had gotten much more infrequent. Part of the reason for that is that I stopped feeling as excited about what I was doing on a day-to-day basis. My guess is that the fun of learning sort of wore off. I used to get a huge dopamine hit whenever I was able to learn something, then turn around and implement it. One side of that was actually finding something fun to learn. I was having a great time learning about AWS and architecture. Looking back, I now see that another side of that dopamine hit was learning how to learn. I haven’t found that “learning high” that I did when I first started as a full-time dev. I have also learned how to learn for the most part, so learning a topic does not seem like such a big deal to me now. It is difficult to find topics that interest you and are new.

Over the past few years, I have dabbled in finding a few hobbies. Again, nothing has given me quite what engineering did when I first started, but I am trying to find new things to learn that are not related to my full-time job.

I started running as an individual sport in junior high. I stopped for a while in college, but I picked it up again near the end of that time. I have since run two marathons, many half marathons, and two triathlons. It is a hobby that keeps me healthy, and there are interesting scientific topics in the sport. I have learned (and am still learning) about different training strategies, aerobic adaptations, and running techniques.

My wife speaks French, and I speak German. We have tried sharing languages with moderate success. Having become completely fluent in a foreign language has definitely helped me start to learn another one. My strategy is painstakingly boring, though, so it has been difficult for me to keep up with any amount of intensity. This topic will be something where consistency will be my best bet to learn because it has been difficult to do more than about 30 minutes of work per day.

My side projects that I have talked about on this blog are mostly still up and active. Not many users for the ones that I have full control over, but I have found success with the apps that I made for a SaaS marketplace. I have hundreds of users, so I feel fulfilled in having created something completely by myself from scratch that other people use.

There is a saying along the lines of “Everybody should have three hobbies. One to keep them healthy, one to keep them wealthy, and one to keep them wise.” The three here complete that as far as I can tell. But that doesn’t mean I’m done looking around. I would like to be able to find something that gets me as passionate as engineering did when I first started full time.

I have also been consistently reading over the past couple of years. The book I have been reading lately is called “Chaos: Charles Manson, the CIA, and the Secret History of the Sixties.”

A friend of mine is into bird watching, and as a result, I have become interested in watching them fly and flit around. Some of the latest birds on my life list are:

* white-winged dove
* spotted towhee
* eastern towhee
* wood duck
* white-throated sparrow
* song sparrow
* great horned owl
* osprey
* red-vented bulbul

My company is in the middle of yearly evaluations, and something I read last year has been coming to my mind over and over. It was a reminder that you have to be your own advocate. The article recommended keeping a “brag list” of sorts about what you have done over the past year or whatever period of time you would like. I started doing that last year, but I was not as consistent as I should have been. Hopefully, I will remain on top of it this year.

Thanks for reading my rambling. I had no real point for this post other than wanting to start writing again.
